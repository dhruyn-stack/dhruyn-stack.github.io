How the eye sees

Light from an object enters the eye through the clear cornea, which bends it the most. The lens then fine-tunes the bend so the light lands sharply on the retina at the back.

Small muscles make the lens fatter for near objects and thinner for far ones. The retina turns light into signals, and the optic nerve carries them to the brain.

Circle it. Copy it.

Hold the title of a card or notebook, then circle any part. Copy it as a picture, or keep it in Clips for any page later.

  1. Hold the title.On a topic card, a notebook or a PDF, hold the title at the top for a moment. The page blurs a little, the edges of the screen glow, and it says “Circle what you want to copy”. You can let go now.

  2. Circle the part you want.Draw a loop around it with your finger or Apple Pencil.

  3. The box snaps to it.It jumps to the whole lines, table cells, tables, pictures and ink inside your circle, and the rest of the page blurs. Drag a corner or an edge to change it. It sticks to the edges on the page.

  4. Tap Copy.A sharp picture of exactly the box goes to the clipboard: “Copied. Paste it anywhere.”

  5. Or tap Add to Clips.The clip waits in Clips (the books button in the toolbar), under the card, notebook or PDF it came from. Search clips finds the words inside, your handwriting too.

  6. Put it on any page.Tap a clip, or drag it onto a notebook page, a topic card or the Canvas. The picture keeps a “From …” link back to where it came from.

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis

Plants make their own food from light. In the leaf, water and carbon dioxide become glucose, a sugar, and oxygen is given off.

The word equation

carbon dioxide + water light, chlorophyll glucose + oxygen

Where it happens

  • In the leaves, inside tiny parts called chloroplasts
  • Chlorophyll, the green colour, traps the light
  • Water comes up from the roots. Carbon dioxide comes in through small holes called stomata

Exam trap

Plants respire all the time, day and night. In bright light, photosynthesis is faster, so the leaf gives off more oxygen than it uses.

Lecture in. Card out.

A card is a study page saved as one HTML file (a web page file whose name ends in .html). Lekhin opens it, and you write on it with Apple Pencil. You don't write any code: an AI chat makes the file for you.

  • Your lecturea PDF, slides or your notes
  • Any AI chatChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or another
  • Lekhinon your iPad
  1. Make the card. Give any AI chat your lecture and the prompt, then download the .html file it makes.
  2. Put it in Lekhin. In the Library, tap + at the top right, then HTML card, and pick the file.
  3. Write on it. Write over the card with Apple Pencil.

1Make the card

  1. Open any AI chat, on your computer or on your iPad.
  2. Attach your lecture PDF, or paste your notes into the chat.
  3. Copy the prompt below and send it with your lecture.
  4. You get back one file whose name ends in .html. Download it. On an iPad, Safari puts it in Files, in the Downloads folder. If the card opens instead, tap Share, then Save to Files.

Only code on the screen? Ask: “Give me this as a file I can download.” On a computer you can also paste the code into Notepad and save it as “card.html”, with the quote marks. On an iPad, ask again or use a computer.

Want changes? Ask in the same chat, for example “Make the tables bigger”, then download the new file. It comes in as a new card, and your writing stays on the old one, so get the card right before you write on it.

The prompt

Turn this lecture into one study card, as a single HTML file I can download. Send me the file itself, not just code in the chat. Name it after the topic. What goes in it: - Use only what is in my lecture, in the same order. Do not add facts or change any number. If something is unclear, keep it and write "Check this" next to it. - Keep the lecture's language. Use short, simple sentences and short points, not long paragraphs. - Put the key facts in bold. Use tables for comparisons and simple drawn diagrams for processes. - End each section with one or two questions, with the answer hidden until it is tapped. Use <details> and <summary> for this. - Leave a few empty boxes where I can write by hand. How to build it: - Put everything inside this one file, saved as UTF-8: the styles in a <style> tag, diagrams as inline SVG, and any pictures from my lecture as data: URIs. If you cannot include a picture, draw it or describe it. Never leave a broken picture. - Plain HTML and CSS only: no scripts, no links to other files or websites, no fonts or pictures from the web. Use fonts an iPad already has. - Put the topic in <title> and in one <h1>. Start each section with an <h2>. Use real tags: <p>, <ul>, <table>, <figure>. - The page is always shown 900 px wide, as one tall page that only scrolls down. Make everything fit that width, with text about 18 px and nothing under 16 px. Do not use position: fixed or sticky, vh units, sideways scrolling, or boxes that scroll inside the page. Let wide tables wrap. - Set every colour as a CSS variable. Put light colours in :root, and dark colours in both :root[data-theme="dark"] and @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { :root:not([data-theme="light"]) { ... } }. - Keep the file under 5 MB. Shrink pictures if you need to.

2Put it in Lekhin

On your iPad. In Lekhin, open the Library or a folder. Tap + at the top right, then HTML card, and pick your file (look in Recents or Downloads). It is copied into that folder and opens. One file at a time, up to 25 MB.

From your PC. Get Card Sync for Windows from the Microsoft Store and drop the file into its window. Your iPad gets it the next time you open Lekhin. About Card Sync

From a Mac. Send the file to your iPad with AirDrop or iCloud Drive, save it to Files, then add it as above.

3Write on it

Write over the card with Apple Pencil. Tap the book button at the top right (My notes) for a notebook that belongs to this card.

To take out a card you added, hold it in the Library or folder where you added it, then tap Remove card. It goes at once. Your file in Files stays.

    Questions

    Answered before you could ask.

    Which iPads does it work on?

    Lekhin Notes is made for iPad only. It runs on any iPad with iPadOS 16 or later, and Apple Pencil is the best way to write. On iPads whose Pencil can hover, the laser pointer follows the Pencil before it even touches the screen.

    Do I need an account?

    Not for your notes. The Study Café signs you in on its own without an email or password, so all you choose is a café name.

    Does it work without the internet?

    Yes. Notebooks, cards, the pens, PDFs and handwriting search all work offline. The café and its voice rooms need the internet, and Card Sync needs your home Wi-Fi.

    Can I bring my notes from another app?

    Yes. Export your notebooks from the old app as PDFs, then use Import PDF in Lekhin and keep writing on them.

    How much will it cost, and when can I get it?

    Lekhin Notes is coming soon to the App Store. The price and the download link will be on this page on launch day.
